Reproducibility record: regional vegetation-treatment study

This record accompanies the Environmental Modelling & Software manuscript.
It records computational settings and identifies the runs used in the tables and figures.
Section 8 describes the fixed-plan sensitivity comparisons.

2. Spatial representation and execution

The minimum treatment area is 20 ha. With 30 m cells of area 0.09 ha, the
integer threshold is 223 cells, or 20.07 ha. Mechanical-treatment patches use
the same threshold.

Planning uses the full available data grids. For example, Pier uses 846 x 873
cells. Its published-procedure IoU replay used a 679 x 706 domain. The model
accuracy assessment and vegetation-planning domain must not be conflated.
The planning windows and masks are recorded in:
  control/results/management_polygon_two_stage_v1/<fire>/inputs/planning/protocol.json

CNN parameter maps are evaluated in float32. CA propagation uses float64.
The CA-CNN implementation clips the receiving and transmission factors to
[0, 100] and the ignition hazard to at most 50. These numerical safeguards come
from the published code. The manuscript omits them from Equation (1).
The runs use the GPU workstation with an NVIDIA RTX 4090 and Intel Core
i9-14900KF processor. Forecast batches contain four candidates, or two on
Ferguson, under a 20 GB GPU-memory ceiling. HiGHS uses at most 20 CPU threads.

Phase 1 can evaluate small removal groups speculatively in batches. Every
acceptance uses a full forecast for the current plan. An accepted removal
invalidates all stored forecasts based on the preceding plan. Phase 2 similarly
reuses exact single-change forecasts only while the current plan is unchanged.

A fixed absolute tolerance of 1e-10 in affected probability is used for the
constraint of each protected cell. It does not accumulate across accepted
changes. Potentially feasible batched results
receive scalar verification before retention. Final plans receive full replay.

3. Search settings

All fires use the settings in algorithm_settings.tex. Phase 2 starts with
Delta = 16, halves it after a block without an accepted change (minimum 1),
and doubles it when an accepted change reaches the limit (maximum 128).
Each round requests up to eight assignments from HiGHS. If a candidate violates an omitted cell constraint, the solver can add constraints and start another round. All rounds in a block share a two-second solver budget.

Seed 73 determines the fixed block permutation and benchmark random draws.
The original randomized block order remains in use. The separate limited Pier
CA-guided ordering experiment did not alter the manuscript's results.

Annealing uses four chains. Move sizes 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 are equally likely.
Each replacement program is sampled uniformly from the other eligible choices.
The initial temperature gives a median positive sampled cost increase an
acceptance probability of 0.8. Geometric cooling reduces the temperature by
1,000 over the budget. Regional infeasibility causes rejection.

NSGA-II uses pymoo with a population of 32 categorical assignments. Uniform
crossover has probability 0.9. Mutation changes one eligible polygon on average
per offspring. It stops at the time budget, at 100,000 generations, or after
50 consecutive generations with an identical population. Feasible plans take
precedence over infeasible plans. The retained answer is the least-cost feasible
plan, not an arbitrary member of the Pareto population.

DPV-ordered removal, our variant of Phase 1, tests groups of at most 128
replacements and subdivides rejected groups. At each destination's first 0.5
crossing, trace the neighbor contributing the largest share of the hazard.
For the time of that neighbor's ignition, compare surviving probability mass
from each prior ignition step, including burning mass at initialization.
Each microstep retains a fraction 1 - 1/tau of each burning cohort. Backtracking these choices gives one
representative route; count each destination once at each spatial cell it visits.
For local program scores, use the untreated burning histories and replace both
fuel multipliers in the local receiving/transmitting hazard by q_io(x). Let
w_n(x,y) be the resulting nonnegative contribution from y, divided by the sum
of incoming contributions at x. A zero sum gives zero weights. Let t_n(y)
be the modal ignition step among the burning probability at y at step n.
At step n+1 the incoming speed is the sum over neighbors y of
w_n(x,y) times distance(x,y) / [(n+1-t_n(y)) times minutes_per_microstep].
The local score sums this speed times the local probability of first ignition
over all forecast steps. Thus failure to ignite contributes zero to the score.
Only the ranking freezes the incoming histories. Every group acceptance uses
the full CA with the actual spatially varying fuels. Each run stores its scores,
arrival map, downstream areas, timing and best verified feasible assignment.

Spread-path placement compares up to eight candidate replacements per selection.
It uses the eight-neighbor Rothermel graph with start-date wind, terrain and
fuel classes. Dead-fuel moisture is 6%, 7% and 8% for one-hour, ten-hour
and hundred-hour fuels, respectively. Live herbaceous and woody fuel moisture
are 60% and 90%. Treatment scales fuel loads at fixed bed depth, surface-area-
to-volume ratios and canopy characteristics. Missing wind components on the
Chimney coastal grid are set to zero, as in the CA preprocessing.

4. Time accounting

The per-fire budget is the sum of the elapsed times to the final cost
improvement in the two phases. A phase without improvement contributes zero.
The shorter comparison uses Phase 1's time to its lowest cost. These are
retrospectively selected budgets, as stated in the paper.

Model setup, compilation and CA path tracing are charged. The spread-path placement
budget also includes the measured time to prepare its Rothermel rates. Final
verification and reporting are recorded separately from timed search.
Pending evaluations and verification can finish after a nominal deadline.
The completed runs of DPV-ordered removal and the benchmarks exceed their
budgets by at most 11.1 s. At the strict deadline spread-path placement
costs $72.748M on Brattain
and $44.558M on Chimney. On Chimney the two cutoffs coincide at 750.263 s.
The same spread-path run retained the $44.549M plan at 750.467 s, including
4.769 s of rate preparation. The same methods rank first at the strict deadline and after final verification.

In Phase 2, all four accepted improvements are single-polygon changes. The
integer subproblem returned one proposal that passed every linearized cell
constraint, in 48 block visits. In 27 visits its candidates violated
constraints already imposed, at a level below the solver tolerance, and the
search of that block ended without a proposal.

Diagnostic after the runs, not credited to the timed method:
  control/results/cellwise_saved_block_logic_check_v1/
On Ferguson, changing two polygons of the final plan together lowers its cost
by $90,265, from $44,655,649 to $44,565,383, and satisfies all 209,651 cell
constraints in a full GPU forecast. One of the two changes alone saves
$141,896 but exceeds one cell limit by 1.23e-10 in probability. The other
alone adds $51,630. A three-polygon candidate saving $273,761 fails at one
cell, by 1.2e-13 beyond the 1e-10 tolerance. Phase 2 did not find the pair
within its search budget.

If Phase 2's deadline occurs within a block, it retains the least-cost feasible
candidate already verified, even if the block has not completed. It does not
promote an unverified integer proposal to the final answer.

5. Supporting STE experiment

The two Pier checks minimize final affected area over the protected union.
They have no monetary penalty or regional bounds. They use the same polygons
and programs as the main study and start from either the strongest reference
or no treatment.

Adam uses learning rate 0.1 and seed 73. Initial logits put the selected choice
near a decision boundary. Objective scaling assigns the reference a value of
10. Hard assignments use the largest eligible logit. The backward calculation
uses the masked softmax derivative and includes CA propagation through burning
cells.

The reference-start run completed 363 updates. Its best result was at update 9:
240.247 ha versus the 240.581 ha reference. The untreated-start run completed
719 updates. Its best result was at update 612: 244.481 ha. Both runs were
stopped manually before the planned 3,000 updates. These were limited empirical
checks, not convergence certificates or proofs of reference optimality.

6. Treatment-response diagnostic

The uniform-landscape diagnostic used eight Pier fuel classes and three wind
speeds. Each 15-day forecast started from a straight front. The affected-
probability contour at 0.5 defined the measured front position. A speed estimate
required at least 20 position samples and an advance beyond 30 cells.
Only four of the 24 untreated cases met the measurement criteria, and none of
the corresponding treated cases did. Failure of these criteria is not evidence
that every remaining case had zero spread. The experiment did not calibrate the
mapping from residual fuel mass to the learned CA coefficient.

8. Fixed-plan sensitivity on four fires

Protocol and aggregate results:
  control/results/ca_transmission_dpv_v1/sensitivity/protocol.json
  control/results/ca_transmission_dpv_v1/sensitivity/summary.json
  control/results/ca_transmission_dpv_v1/sensitivity/comparison.csv
Script:
  control/experiments/run_cellwise_sensitivity.py
  control/experiments/replay_ca_guided_dpv_sensitivity.py

Each fire retains three nominal plans: the strongest-treatment reference,
the final two-phase plan, and the DPV-ordered removal plan reported in the
benchmark comparison. Their assignments, costs, forecast model, initial fire,
weather, asset cores, 150 m bands and nominal cell limits remain fixed.
The four response scenarios change only grass or shrub treatment multipliers.
No optimization is performed in this sensitivity assessment.

Grass scenarios consume 95% / 100% or 80% / 90% of herbaceous / one-hour dead
fuel, respectively. These are endpoints from CARB's October 16, 2025 webinar,
slide 62. Coarse dead and live woody loads retain their nominal assumptions.
Shrub residual fractions are 0.17 and 0.50, motivated by Hardy et al., Table 3,
and Green, Figure 1. These transferred scenarios are not confidence intervals.
Forest responses remain fixed. Source URLs and assumptions are in the protocol.

The comparison uses 40 saved GPU forecasts for the reference and two-phase plans
and 20 GPU replays of the CA-guided DPV plans. The three plans for each fire are
its reference, its final two-phase plan and its DPV-ordered removal plan. The
nominal forecasts are checked against the saved plan maps to absolute tolerance
1e-10, and each plan's cost is checked against its saved cost. Finiteness and
the range of every forecast are checked.

The comparison has 60 rows: four fires, three plans, and five response settings
including nominal. Costs remain constant across settings for each plan.
Affected asset hectares count overlapping asset cells once. Violations count
the protected cells whose affected probability exceeds its nominal limit by
more than 1e-10. No scenario-specific reference limit replaces the nominal
limit. Per-asset details are in each fire's assets.csv. Source hashes identify
the fixed plans, fuel fields, model maps, masks and scripts.

The journal's prepare_sensitivity_table.py generates sensitivity_results.tex
and sensitivity_provenance.json from the completed three-plan results.
The earlier fixed-plan study of the regional plans, and the reoptimization and
band-width experiments, remain archived in their original results directories
and do not supply the sensitivity table.

9. Daily arrival and probability checks on the reported plans

The post-run check in control/results/cellwise_arrival_check_v1/check.py reads
the saved daily maps for the two-phase method and literature benchmarks. For
the CA-guided DPV plans, see sensitivity/dpv_arrival_summary.json and each
fire's dpv_asset_arrivals.csv under control/results/ca_transmission_dpv_v1/sensitivity/.
The asset arrival tables give the first day any
cell inside each asset core reaches affected probability 0.5, including the
initial map as day 0. daily_probability_check.csv compares each protected cell
(including the 150 m band) with the same cell under the reference on days 1
through 15. The reported maximum is taken over the daily differences.
