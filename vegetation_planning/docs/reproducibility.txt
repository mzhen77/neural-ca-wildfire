Reproducibility record: regional vegetation-treatment study

This record accompanies the Environmental Modelling & Software manuscript.
It preserves execution details removed from the narrative. The manuscript's
model, treatment responses, algorithms and comparison rules are unchanged.
The four-fire results remain unchanged. The additional Pier sensitivity study
is described in item 8 below.

2. Spatial representation and execution

The minimum treatment area is 20 ha. With 30 m cells of area 0.09 ha, the
integer threshold is 223 cells, or 20.07 ha. Mechanical-treatment patches use
the same threshold.

Planning uses the full available data grids. For example, Pier uses 846 x 873
cells. Its published-procedure IoU replay used a 679 x 706 domain. The model
accuracy assessment and vegetation-planning domain must not be conflated.
The planning windows and masks are recorded in:
  control/results/management_polygon_two_stage_v1/<fire>/inputs/planning/protocol.json

CNN parameter maps are evaluated in float32. CA propagation uses float64.
The runs use the GPU workstation with an NVIDIA RTX 4090 and Intel Core
i9-14900KF processor. Forecast batches contain four candidates, or two on
Ferguson, under a 20 GB GPU-memory ceiling. HiGHS uses at most 20 CPU threads.

Phase 1 can evaluate small removal groups speculatively in batches. Every
acceptance uses a full forecast for the current plan. An accepted removal
invalidates all stored forecasts based on the preceding plan. Phase 2 similarly
reuses exact single-change forecasts only while the current plan is unchanged.

A fixed absolute tolerance of 1e-8 ha is used for each regional bound. It does
not accumulate across accepted changes. Potentially feasible batched results
receive scalar verification before retention. Final plans receive full replay.

3. Search settings

All fires use the settings in algorithm_settings.tex. Phase 2 starts with
Delta = 16, halves it after a block without an accepted change (minimum 1),
and doubles it when an accepted change reaches the limit (maximum 128).
The eight HiGHS proposals share one two-second allowance per block.

Seed 73 determines the fixed block permutation and benchmark random draws.
The original randomized block order remains in use. The separate limited Pier
CA-guided ordering experiment did not alter the manuscript's results.

Annealing uses four chains. Move sizes 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 are equally likely.
Each replacement program is sampled uniformly from the other eligible choices.
The initial temperature gives a median positive sampled cost increase an
acceptance probability of 0.8. Geometric cooling reduces the temperature by
1,000 over the allowance. Regional infeasibility causes rejection.

NSGA-II uses pymoo with a population of 32 categorical assignments. Uniform
crossover has probability 0.9. Mutation changes one eligible polygon on average
per offspring. It stops at the time allowance, at 100,000 generations, or after
50 consecutive generations with an identical population. Feasible plans take
precedence over infeasible plans. The retained answer is the least-cost feasible
plan, not an arbitrary member of the Pareto population.

DPV tests groups of at most 128 replacements and subdivides rejected groups.
Spread-path placement compares up to eight candidate replacements per selection.
The eight-neighbor propagation graph is a separate model assumption, described
in the manuscript.

4. Time accounting

The per-fire allowance is the sum of the elapsed times to the final cost
improvement in the two phases. A phase without improvement contributes zero.
The shorter comparison uses Phase 1's time to its lowest cost. These are
retrospectively selected allowances, as stated in the paper.

Model setup and JIT compilation are charged. Path methods also receive their
measured rate-preparation charge when stored rate fields are reused. Final
verification and reporting are recorded separately from timed search.
Pending evaluations and verification can finish after a nominal deadline.
The completed benchmark runs exceed their allowances by at most 10.8 s.
At the strict deadline, Ferguson DPV costs $43.012M. The strict-deadline
comparison preserves the reported rankings.

If Phase 2's deadline occurs within a block, it retains the least-cost feasible
candidate already verified, even if the block has not completed. It does not
promote an unverified integer proposal to the final answer.

5. Supporting STE experiment

The two Pier checks minimize final affected area over the protected union.
They have no monetary penalty or regional bounds. They use the same polygons
and programs as the main study and start from either the strongest reference
or no treatment.

Adam uses learning rate 0.1 and seed 73. Initial logits put the selected choice
near a decision boundary. Objective scaling assigns the reference a value of
10. Hard assignments use the largest eligible logit. The backward calculation
uses the masked softmax derivative and includes CA propagation through burning
cells.

The reference-start run completed 363 updates. Its best result was at update 9:
240.247 ha versus the 240.581 ha reference. The untreated-start run completed
719 updates. Its best result was at update 612: 244.481 ha. Both runs were
stopped manually before the planned 3,000 updates. These were limited empirical
checks, not convergence certificates or proofs of reference optimality.

6. Treatment-response diagnostic

The uniform-landscape diagnostic used eight Pier fuel classes and three wind
speeds. Each 15-day forecast started from a straight front. The affected-
probability contour at 0.5 defined the measured front position. A speed estimate
required at least 20 position samples and an advance beyond 30 cells.
Only four of the 24 untreated cases met the measurement criteria, and none of
the corresponding treated cases did. Failure of these criteria is not evidence
that every remaining case had zero spread. The experiment did not calibrate the
mapping from residual fuel mass to the learned CA coefficient.

8. Pier sensitivity study

Configuration and source evidence:
  control/configs/vegetation_sensitivity_pier_v1.json
Preparation, sequential GPU queue and live summaries:
  control/experiments/run_vegetation_sensitivity.py
Results:
  control/results/vegetation_sensitivity_pier_v1/

The six scenarios separately change grass consumption (two endpoint pairs),
shrub residual mass (0.17 or 0.5), or band width (100 or 200 m). They preserve
the nominal four-fire study. Repeated baseline GPU forecasts reproduce its
saved Pier histories exactly. Each changed response is applied to the
vegetation-specific operation within each existing whole-polygon program.
The scenario changes neither the checkpoint nor the CNN input maps.

The grass endpoints come from CARB's October 16, 2025 inventory webinar,
slide 62. They are inventory assumptions rather than measured uncertainty
limits. The 0.17 shrub response is a measured site mean from Hardy et al.,
Table 3. The 0.5 response is a broader scenario motivated by Green's Figure 1
caption. It is not an estimated Pier response or a confidence interval.

All scenarios use the same assets and polygon inventory. Bands use Euclidean
cell-centre distance on the 30 m grid. Thirteen of Pier's 308 asset features
reach the simulation boundary under each tested band, so their surroundings
are represented only within the available domain. Each scenario recomputes
all reference bounds with no percentage slack. The nominal selected plan is
replayed before reoptimization, allowing changes in its protection to be
distinguished from changes after the planner adapts.

Phase 1 completes its ordered removal pass. Phase 2 has the existing 3,600 s
allowance and can terminate on an unchanged sweep. The queue runs one GPU
process at a time and preserves saved source hashes. The nominal baseline
results are reused after their exact replay check. These runs are scenario
comparisons and do not repeat the four literature benchmarks.

All six scenarios completed and satisfy every regional bound. Every Phase 2
run terminated after a complete unchanged sweep. comparison.csv records both
phases, and nominal_replay.json supplies the nominal row. Run the journal's
prepare_sensitivity_table.py to generate sensitivity_results.tex and its source
hashes in sensitivity_provenance.json.

The 100 m run required recovery after final verification used a separately
compiled history forecast. Differences of order 1e-13 ha crossed the 1e-8 ha
feasibility tolerance. Final verification now uses the identical compiled
scalar forecast used for acceptance and independently checks saved histories.
Five repeated scalar GPU forecasts passed for the retained 100 m plan. The
assignment and tolerance were unchanged. The saved diagnostic is
band_100m/scalar_verification.json. Failed events and recovery records remain
in the logs. timing.json excludes recovery diagnostics and idle time.
